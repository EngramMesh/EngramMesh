# EngramMesh Governance

EngramMesh is stewarded in the open by the `@EngramMesh/maintainers` team for the `EngramMesh/EngramMesh` repository. Decisions should prioritize the project's documented architecture, contributor safety, and long-term maintainability.

## Roles

### Contributor

A Contributor proposes issues, RFCs, documentation, tests, or code and follows the contribution and conduct policies. Contributors may participate in public discussion and review.

### Reviewer

A Reviewer provides substantive, respectful feedback on pull requests, ADRs, or RFCs. Reviewers help verify correctness, tests, documentation, compatibility, and policy compliance; they do not merge changes solely by virtue of this role.

### Maintainer

Maintainers are members of `@EngramMesh/maintainers`. They facilitate public decision-making, review and merge contributions, enforce project policies, and maintain the repository's security and release responsibilities.

## Role advancement, departure, and removal

Role changes are made in public. A nomination for Reviewer or Maintainer must identify the proposed role, include the candidate's explicit consent, and link evidence of sustained contributions appropriate to that role (for example, reviews, issues, RFCs, documentation, tests, or code). The nomination remains open for public comment for at least 14 calendar days. The resulting decision, approvals, and a concise rationale are recorded on the nomination issue or an associated public decision record.

After the comment window closes, a Reviewer appointment requires approval from at least two active, non-conflicted Maintainers. A Maintainer appointment requires approval from at least two active, non-conflicted Maintainers other than the candidate. A nominator or candidate with a conflict of interest must recuse from moderation, deliberation, and approval. The general conflict rule below applies to every Maintainer approval and threshold in this process.

An initial bootstrap or a period with fewer than two active Maintainers does not permit one person to make a permanent role, repository-access, or other authority change. In that case, the normal appointment and removal thresholds remain in force. If continuity requires a permanent recovery action, it must instead use a public RFC with a 14-calendar-day comment window and approval by at least three non-conflicted Established Contributors, with at least two-thirds voting in favor; the candidate does not vote. An Established Contributor is a person with at least three accepted contributions in the preceding 12 months. If this recovery threshold cannot be met, permanent authority changes are deferred while the project seeks additional independent contributors.

Maintainers may voluntarily depart or move to emeritus status by posting a public notice. Emeritus Maintainers retain recognition but no active repository authority. Reinstatement follows the applicable public appointment process for the role, including the same evidence, comment window, recusal, and approval requirements.

A Reviewer or Maintainer may be removed for material or repeated policy or Code of Conduct violations, misuse or neglect of repository or security responsibilities, sustained unavailability that prevents safe stewardship, or conduct that creates a material legal, security, or community-safety risk. The proposed removal must provide notice, the grounds and available supporting evidence, and at least 14 calendar days for the person to respond before a decision. The subject, complainant, and conflicted Maintainers must recuse. Removal requires approval from at least two active, non-conflicted Maintainers other than the subject; if that threshold cannot be met without conflicted Maintainers, the decision is deferred. The outcome and rationale are recorded publicly.

When there is a credible, immediate risk to repository security, contributor safety, or release integrity, one non-conflicted Maintainer may temporarily suspend access. The suspension must be limited to the necessary access, recorded publicly as soon as safely possible, and expires after seven calendar days unless ratified within 72 hours by two active, non-conflicted Maintainers other than the person whose access was suspended. If it is not ratified, access is restored promptly. A ratified suspension lasts only while an active removal proceeding is pending, must be reviewed at least every seven calendar days, and must end immediately with access restored if the proceeding closes without removal or is abandoned. It remains temporary and must proceed through the removal process before any permanent authority or access change.

Public records may redact personal information and sensitive security or conduct details. They must nevertheless state the outcome, the applicable process, and as much rationale as can be safely disclosed.

## Decisions and approvals

Project decisions are made through issues, pull requests, ADRs, RFCs, and published meeting notes, with the most durable decision record linked from the change. Ordinary changes require appropriate review by a Maintainer.

Changes involving licensing, a security boundary, compatibility, or governance require approval from two Maintainers before merge or adoption. Where an RFC is required, the approval is recorded on its public discussion or implementing pull request.

Maintainers must disclose actual, potential, or perceived conflicts of interest that could materially affect a decision. A materially conflicted Maintainer must recuse from moderation, deliberation, and approval, and must never be counted toward any required threshold. If the non-conflicted threshold cannot be met, the decision is deferred.

## Stewardship and continuity

Maintainer activity is reviewed after 180 days without visible project participation. The review is an opportunity to confirm continued availability, update access, and invite the Maintainer to resume participation when ready. Any resulting role or access change follows the role advancement, departure, and removal process above.

Project decisions made in meetings must have public meeting notes, including the decision, participants, rationale, and links to any relevant issue, ADR, or RFC. Sensitive security details may be redacted while preserving a public record of the decision and rationale to the extent safely possible.
